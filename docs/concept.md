# Product concept log

The `smudge-concept` panel (`.claude/agents/smudge-concept.md`) reads this file at the start of
every session and proposes lines for it at the end. Muhammad decides what goes in.

There are two separate products. Keep their ideas apart.

1. **Smudge**: your page, the first thing you open; every page runs programs, and programs connect
   people. Built to replace Instagram, not to complement it.
2. **Bubble**: a nested Reddit with a world map of interest bubbles you can step into.

## 1. Smudge

### Direction: your page, the first thing you open (Oct 2026)

The center is the page. Moments (weddings, reunions, trips, dinners) are one program on it, not
the core and not the entry door. Smudge is a replacement for Instagram, not a complement. "Home"
is only an internal word; to users it is their page.

**Macro, one line each (Oct 25):**

1. **Problem:** people open the phone without a purpose, and today reels catch that moment.
2. **Answer:** Smudge is the first thing you open when you don't know what to do, with everything
   you usually do already there.
3. **Your own place (strongest):** a page you arrange for your own use, your music one tap away.
4. **Other people (the rest):** seeing what friends are up to, through a normal feed and scroll.
5. **Form:** every page is HTML, so it holds programs you use, not only photos you look at.
6. **Compression vs decompression:** Instagram shows a person through photos and numbers; Smudge
   shows who they are.
7. **Two values:** good to use alone long term, and makes others curious and envious.
8. **Business model:** feed, scroll and ads as on any social network, plus themes, memberships,
   early releases and commissions inside.
9. **Entry door:** young people, worldwide, through things that go viral and are easy to make.
   Money is not the constraint; spend it on spotlight, density and content, not install ads.
10. **Ordinary users are the core:** without them creators and businesses have no reason to come.
11. **Creators:** people first, then money; small hungry creators, by merit, from their closest
    fans; no guaranteed money, no creator program at the start.
12. **Fan interaction:** what only HTML can do (rooms unlocked together, traces left, a page that
    remembers each fan), plus pay-for-early-access for any digital work.
13. **Businesses:** they need a way to sell; they meet users through products people choose to
    display on their pages.
14. **Smudge's job:** tools, plus what the law requires. Communities and economies belong to
    creators and businesses.
15. **Product moat, one line:** pages that run programs, and programs that connect people.
    Everything else (archive, proof, early release, embeds, distribution) derives from it.
16. **HTML is a bet on openness:** users never feel it; it pays off only when programs open to
    other makers, in stages, behind a strict sandbox.
17. **Security:** most risks close by design from day one; the human ones (phishing by words,
    bad content, bullying) need moderation and process, as on Instagram.
18. **What matters now:** design and execution.

**Your page:**

- One full web page per person, designed by the owner. Not a photo grid, not a literal house
  (a house can be one theme, never the base form). To users it is "your page"; "home" and
  "rooms" are internal words only, because the metaphor means nothing to normies.
- The page has a public part (what anyone sees) and private parts (the owner's own, where
  friends enter only when invited). Access per part is on by default, not an extra feature.
- The page is built in about 60 seconds from a template plus guiding questions; nobody writes
  code. Programs on it follow the page's theme like themed widgets, never clip art on top.
- Things on a page: a theme (colours, type, texture, motion), entry music, a "what I'm up to"
  line, a player, collections, top friends, a guestbook and wall for visitors' stickers and
  doodles, quizzes and small games, rare items from events or gifts, a pet that grows with
  visits. The page changes because of other people, traces pile up, and that is why it is hard
  to leave.
- The page opens anywhere without an account, can be the link in a bio, and can be embedded
  outside Smudge. Pages are also the distribution channel: whatever people attach to their page
  (a song, a comic, a program) spreads from page to page.

**The place for yourself (the strongest value):**

- The need: people open the phone without a purpose and want something to catch that moment.
  Today reels catch it because they ask for no decision.
- Smudge is the first thing you open when you don't know what to do. Everything you usually do
  is already there, arranged by you: your music one tap away, what you are doing noted in one
  tap and piling up into an archive, friends' feed, chat, more to define. No thinking, no
  switching apps. The rival is the reflex of opening reels, not Instagram.
- What fills it first: small one-purpose programs of the kind that already went viral because
  the result is worth showing off (NGL, visited-places maps like Been and Polarsteps, Spotify
  Wrapped, Receiptify, Letterboxd's four favourites, MySpace top 8, "how well do you know me"
  quizzes, countdowns, guestbooks). These are examples of the kind, not a list to copy. Each
  such app died because it had one function; here they are ready-made programs on one page,
  permanent, themed, connected to each other. Results must travel to Instagram stories easily.
- Also in: a home-screen widget showing one friend's page live, tapped before reels can open;
  shared pages owned by two or more people (a couple, a trip, a class); a study-or-work-together
  part of the page (friends' small avatars, a 25-minute timer, the same music, no chat) that
  stays usable alone. Order: widget, shared pages, study room.
- Open: what exactly people do alone there without it becoming a notes app; zero decisions on
  the first screen; access to Spotify, WhatsApp and the like.

**Friends (the rest):**

- Seeing what friends are up to, through a normal feed and scroll, and tapping in to join
  (listen along, play their quiz, feed the shared pet).
- Programs that span pages are what pull friends in, because their value is zero unless friends
  are here. Reference that passed the visual filter: Rina's page as a designed zine (cream, big
  serif, taped photo), a small player with her song, and in the corner a pixel cat "owned by
  Rina & Dika, 23 days old" that lives on both pages, fed by either, runs away if both forget.
  People care because it is about them and one specific person, visible to everyone, and can
  die (Widgetable and Locket prove it).
- Ordinary users come because they are curious about a friend and stay because the page is
  comfortable for themselves and friends are there. Being more comfortable here than on
  Instagram is what matters; trying new viral things is the hook, not the reason to stay.

**Creators:**

- Who: content creators, artists and public figures. Mainly small, hungry creators who want to
  become stars (as TikTok did), not paid celebrities. Famous people are welcome and bring the
  crowd, but they are not bought.
- People first, money second. If the page wins for them as ordinary users, they move in as
  people (Lisa buying Labubu because she liked it); money is a layer on top. No creator program
  at the start, no guaranteed money, no selling themes as their income. By merit: those who come
  first get the crowd.
- The unserved space is depth, not reach. Instagram and TikTok serve reach; Smudge is where the
  most loyal fans gather with the creator, monetised from the first fan. Instagram Subscriptions
  sells exclusive content one way; Smudge sells a place to be together.
- Pay for early access, for any digital work: release here first, members pay, exclusive about
  3 months, then public, and the work can be attached to members' pages so it spreads. Songs
  first (Spotify pays about $3-5 per 1,000 streams; 300 paying fans beat that), then comics by
  chapter, small HTML games, episodes, serial books, presets, lessons. What is sold is the
  membership (early, "fan since", closeness), not the file, because files leak within a day.
  Discovery stays free. Label contracts limit known artists; independents are the fallback.
- Fan interaction is where HTML wins: a page that remembers each fan, a secret part the fans
  unlock together, shared walls, listening together, locked letters, "I was there" proof,
  interactive release stories, and rituals of leaving a mark moved from real life (drawing
  walls, pin maps, wishing trees, candles, time capsules). Check each ritual: who leaves it, for
  whom, and is it normal in real life (a love lock for an idol is weird; a support lock "Rina,
  since 2026" is not). Being seen back: the idol can visit fans' pages, because fans have pages.
- Expectation sets value (satisfaction = what you get minus what you expected): a small posted
  promise plus surprises. Low-effort delivery: automatic presence, one action reaching many, five
  minutes of liking fan traces, a posted rhythm ("drops by every Friday"), the community itself.
- Roles: the creator keeps the promise; fan organisers appointed by the creator run daily life;
  Smudge keeps safety. Whether organisers are paid is the creator's business, as on YouTube.
- Tools that strengthen creators: updates that reach the whole fan group with no algorithm cut
  (hard for Instagram, whose ads depend on algorithm control), a fan map by city, fan-funded
  projects, creator neighbours for discovery, a living media kit.
- Packs per creator type, because closeness looks different: footballers, musicians, anime
  creators and illustrators, films and studios (an explorable world, puzzles that unlock the
  trailer, a cinema QR sticker; studios paying for opt-in campaign packs is money that is not
  ads). Start with one or two types; proposed: musicians.

**Businesses:** opportunistic; they need a way to sell, not just a place. They meet users
naturally when a friend or creator chooses to display a product on their page: tap to buy, the
one who displayed it earns a commission. Only the page owner can display; no paid slots, no
boosting in the friends' feed. Feed, scroll and ads exist as on any social network.

**Moat and HTML:**

- Moat in the direction: counter-positioning. Instagram cannot force 2 billion people to switch
  from showing off photos to putting themselves together.
- Product moat, one line: pages that run programs, and programs that connect people. Archive,
  proof, early release, embeds and distribution derive from it. Until the core activity that is
  only possible here exists in the product, this is a good idea, not a moat.
- Why HTML: users never feel it; they feel "this page can be anything and can be used". HTML's
  advantage is on our side: supply of makers (anyone, including AI from one sentence), distribution
  (opens anywhere, embeds, searchable) and unlimited form. It pays off only when programs open to
  other makers. Open in stages: our programs first, people customise through themes and options;
  then selected makers with review, like Shopify; then everyone, in a tested sandbox, with a clear
  trigger for each stage.
- What a social profile cannot do today and an HTML page can: differ per visitor; run while you
  are there; be changed permanently by visitors; hold many people at once; take any form; hold
  things that grow or die; hide parts unlocked by puzzles, dates or proof; embed anything;
  connect to other pages; open anywhere without an account. Each exists somewhere (Notion,
  Carrd, Discord, Roblox); none on the social profile friends open daily.
- Security: most risks close by design from day one (no network from programs, identity and
  payment only in the host, one origin per program, resource budgets, few visible permissions,
  frozen program versions, report and delete in two taps, closed defaults). Human risks
  (phishing by words, bad content, bullying) need moderation and process, as on Instagram.

**Open:**

- Which young people first (school, university, young workers), which country first, web first
  or native (the widget needs native), and which creator type first.
- The one-line for normies. Last proposal: "Lagi ngapain, catet di sini."
- Moderation at scale for drawings and traces; finding your own mark among a million.
- Fan mechanics must not become gambling: never tie surprises or rare items to payment; limit
  trading. A "this is really them" signal when managers or bots act for a creator.
- Big studios asking for custom work (the agency trap); campaigns stay opt-in on the studio's
  own page.

### Moments, as one program on the page

Built in stages 0 to 5 and kept. It is one use of the page among many, useful for weddings and
any gathering, never the only one and not the entry door.

- A moment is shaped like a Telegram group: the invite pinned on top, a chat tab, an album tab,
  small groups inside. It closes after the event and becomes one memory card on each attendee's
  page. Only people who were there are in it; outsiders see nothing unless the host opens it.
- Wedding flow: template, vendors attach, link goes to WhatsApp and opens without an account,
  RSVP by phone number, QR at the door replaces the guestbook and opens the live album, the same
  link becomes album plus thank-you afterwards. Album chapters follow the rundown, photographer
  and most-liked photos on top, "your photos" from tags, locked a few days after the hosts
  rearrange. Picked photos can be posted to the page and friends' feeds.
- Places: venues, restaurants, cafes and vendors can have a page with stamps and reviews from
  people proven to have come. One QR scan ties the place, the proof and the album.
- Proof of presence from these scans feeds the visited map and proof-locked themes.
- WhatsApp can copy the planning but not the after: no pages, no timeline, and end-to-end
  encryption stops it arranging 300 guests' photos. Moments never ask anyone to leave WhatsApp.

### Not doing

- Food delivery, being a bank or Stripe, store backend, pay-per-visit as the main billing.
- Replacing WhatsApp; it stays. Paid verification; selling or licensing people's data.

## 2. Bubble

### What it is

- Premise: everyone lives inside interest bubbles, and today those bubbles are invisible. You
  cannot see how big a bubble is, whether it is growing, where it is busy, or its mood, nor which
  bubbles you are in.
- Form: a nested feed like Reddit (bubbles inside bubbles), posting free like Reddit. The feed is
  what people open daily.
- The one thing that differs: a world map of bubbles. Size from people, color from mood, glow when
  growing; zoom follows the nesting (Games → Soulslike → Elden Ring). A private personal map shows
  which bubbles you are in, shareable like Wrapped.
- The map is a door, not a picture: tap a country to enter that country's feed of the bubble,
  auto-translated ("what Koreans think of this patch"), or jump to another bubble's view of the same
  thing (runners and sneakerheads on one shoe). Communities are split by language and app today
  (Reddit, X, 5ch, Bilibili, DC Inside); nowhere shows how the rest of the world talks about the
  same thing.
- One line: "See the world as bubbles, and find yours."

### Features on the map

- Trending counts real people, not posts, per bubble and per country, and measures change (growth,
  mood shift) rather than volume. Unlike Twitter, bots and news do not dominate.
- Country page: top songs, most played games, games made there, and its people's feed. Every chart
  is a door into the conversation.
- Live reaction: during a World Cup final, a big release or a patch, the map moves and you can enter
  any country's feed. This is the main puller.
- Bridges: bilingual people who explain context between two bubbles get known for it.
- Replay: a time slider showing how a bubble grew or how its mood changed.
- Context card: for a person or topic you keep hearing about but do not know, show which bubble it
  comes from, why it is known (explained from inside), a sourced timeline, and the bubble's mood.
  "TikTok gives the fragment, Bubble gives the whole picture."

### Prestige, gurus and booking

- A stamp matters only if it is hard, recognised by people (not just the system), and sits on a
  visible ladder. Model: the World Marathon Majors Six Star medal; Sydney's push to join shows
  issuers want into a good bracket because it sends people to them, which solves the issuer
  chicken-and-egg. We curate brackets; issuers pay to join.
- Few brackets: prestige only needs to be read inside its own bubble, so one Slam per bubble, only
  bubbles big and old enough earn one, and if more than about 1% finish, next year gets harder.
- Slam: a permanent name with contents renewed each year (like the Ballon d'Or), because games keep
  changing. Example: "Soulslike Slam 2026", the 4 hardest soulslikes of that year. Old Slams become
  veteran marks. Calendar: picks announced at the start of the year, chase during it, finishers at
  the end. Studios cannot pay to get in; picks by community or a fixed rule (lowest completion rate).
- What the holder gets: identity that travels (Bubble name, Smudge theme unlock, CV), a closed
  circle of holders, and the next rung. Holders are the "one person who pulls 1000".
- Radical layer, prestige as power rather than decoration:
  - Academy: holders pick next year's Slam and vote the bubble's awards ("Soulslike of the Year"
    by proven players, not journalists); winners put the laurel on their cover.
  - Belts that can be taken: each bubble has a belt holder per country, challengers can take it,
    and the map shows who holds what. Gives daily return.
  - Lineage: a title is granted by someone who holds it, their name stays on yours, and they share
    blame for cheating (as in jiu-jitsu).
  - Country against country: everyone's titles add to a national score; only matters with a real
    consequence (the top country hosts next year's Slam Invitational, like Eurovision). Otherwise a
    marketing moment, not the core.
  - Group titles: clubs and guilds chase a Slam together, a reason to invite friends.
- Guru: prestige for making others succeed. Bubble links advice to proven outcomes ("your tips
  helped 2,300 people beat Malenia"); others can only count likes.
- Booking: pay someone for a real-time consult or lesson, priced by legitimacy (proven results)
  more than fame. Unlike ADPList or Topmate, mentors are chosen by outcome, not self-filled
  profiles. Likely Bubble's main money: a marketplace cut. The guru's outcome count only grows on
  sessions booked through Bubble, which keeps them from leaving to WA.
- Booking is narrow: general tips are free on YouTube, so people only pay for what is about them:
  someone reviewing how they play or move (ranked replays, running form, boxing form), a plan for
  their situation, and life decisions (university, career, joining a team). YouTube creators can
  link videos and earn guru credit, so YouTube is a source, not a rival.
- Results are used to choose a guru, not to pay one: payment stays per session; across many
  students the outcome count stays honest.
- Curator: for bubbles without achievements (film, books, music), prove taste instead. Predictions
  recorded before the result (review scores, box office, award winners, artists found early) build
  a track record ("right on 78% of 200 films"). Curators can join the academy and be booked. No
  money stakes (not gambling); guesses hidden until the result; only for public outcomes.
- Rival: for the middle, not the top. Bubble pairs you with one person in another country at
  exactly your level by proof; weekly progress, peek at their country's feed, a stronger rival when
  you pass them. Replace inactive rivals fast; talking is optional, numbers by default.
- AI from proven paths: an assistant trained on thousands of real journeys to success, answering
  from people in the same position who made it. Data grows with every success. Subscription; the AI
  covers the common path and can recommend a guru for the personal one.
- Money from Slams: a small season entry fee (tracking, race number, physical medal; finishing
  never bought, a free route stays) and a yearly Slam Invitational for finishers (broadcast,
  sponsors, tickets), like Boston qualifying.

### Moat and money

- The map can be copied; it is the marketing. The moat is people from many countries already in one
  place, which no regional platform gets by adding a feature.
- Money: official bubbles free; studios and brands pay for crowd and mood insight; countries
  (cultural agencies, tourism boards) and local game makers pay for an official country page, marked
  official and kept apart from people's posts.
- Verified by achievement started as a Smudge idea (theme unlocks). Bubble may use it to keep
  crowds real and weigh answers, but it is not the core.

### Open

- Prestige: lineage and belts need a form of challenge in bubbles without head-to-head (film,
  books); academy needs term limits and secret votes against cliques and bribes; booking needs a
  rule for splitting credit between several gurus and a guard against account boosting in games;
  AI data needs consent and aggregates only.
- Translation of community slang; cold start per country (start with 2 or 3 countries big in one
  bubble); outsiders flooding another country's feed (maybe read-only first).
- Context cards about real people: defamation risk, gossip drift, accuracy while a story is hot.
- Privacy: aggregates only, no slice under about 100 people, rough country only, personal map
  private by default.
- Chart data from Spotify or Steam may not be licensed; move to Bubble's own activity over time.
- Paid campaigns and state pages must be marked and kept out of trending.

## Other ideas, not yet a product

- A proof wallet as its own product used by both Smudge and Bubble.
- Reviews only from people who scanned in (overlaps Smudge businesses).
- A standalone context card as the cheapest test of Bubble's premise.
- A "really experienced it" mark other sites (marketplaces, maps, stores) can add against fake
  reviews. Weak: big platforms can build it themselves.

### B2B: selling proof of who really did what

- Research panels of real people (fastest): survey respondents are proven to be the right people
  (bots and fake "gamers" are research's biggest problem), and behavior beats stated opinion (says
  they hate the patch, still plays daily). Strong in games, sport and places; weak where proof is
  weak (film, books). Food works through places: Smudge stamps show who ate where and who came
  back (return rate is the honest taste signal); taste itself via tasting sessions for proven fans;
  packaged food via receipt scans (Fetch already does this). Data comes from both products, so where
  it lives is undecided.
- Hiring by proof instead of CVs (biggest, slowest): CVs are AI-written and applications flood in;
  employers search for proven work (open-source contributions, finished bootcamps) and vouches
  from proven people. LinkedIn's market (billions a year from companies; rough, unchecked). Hard:
  HR habits, anti-discrimination rules, access to work data.
- Concert tickets for real fans: presale priority for proven fans (listening hours, past concerts,
  time in the bubble) against bots and scalpers, as in Jakarta's Coldplay ticket war. Ticketmaster's
  Verified Fan checks human, not fan. Sold to promoters per ticket. Hard: Ticketmaster exclusive
  contracts, Spotify data access, fairness to new fans.
- Location data for opening stores: not only how many pass (Placer.ai does this from phone
  location) but what kind of people, from consented Smudge scans. Needs many places on our QR first.

## Shared rules

- Method: human desire, then context and goal (fixed), then the way (radical), then whether the
  difference matters or is sugarcoating. Think top down from maximum potential.
- A feature Instagram can paste on without breaking its machine is not a weapon.
- The toy has to be about the person, not about the toy.

## Rejected

- Starting from a niche (run clubs) as the goal. A niche may only come back as an entry tactic
  under agenda item 7.
- "Don't compete with Discord" as a reason to close off an idea.
- Deep research reports for these questions: no meaningful information.
- Guaranteed money for creators up front: it rents loyalty that leaves when the money stops, and
  the burn has no ceiling. Creators earn by merit instead.
- Selling themes as creators' main income: not how creators or public figures make money.
- Smudge solving creators' community problems for them (paying moderators, running their
  economy): Smudge gives tools; the rest is the creator's job.
- Weddings in Indonesia as the entry door, and moments as the core: replaced by the page and
  young people, worldwide. Moments stay as one program.
- "A complement to Instagram": Smudge replaces Instagram.
- Mixing Smudge and Bubble into one product.
- Pay if it works (money held until a result is proven), for gurus and as its own product: results
  depend on too many things outside the provider's control (the student's effort, injury, time),
  unlike a delivered IT project, so good providers would leave.
